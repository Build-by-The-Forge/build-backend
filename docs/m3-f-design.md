# M3-F design: conversations and cited chat

**Status: proposed, for the reviewer. Nothing here is implemented.** Written against backend `a16db6d` and ai-service `0553f35`. M3-F owns `/ai/conversations` in `openapi.yaml`.

**Summary:** students get conversations the backend owns, with answers stored only once the AI step succeeds, and only with citations the backend has checked. The persistence and routes can be built now. What an answer may draw on waits for M3-D's scope filter and then M4. M3-F also defines what happens to past answers when a document they cite is deleted, as the reviewer ruled on M3-D.

## Decisions for the reviewer

| # | Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A repeat of a send with the same key while the first is still waiting for the AI service | Answer 409 `TURN_IN_PROGRESS` with `Retry-After`, a contract addition. Holding a database transaction open across a 60-second AI call isn't acceptable |
| 2 | Past answers that cite a document since deleted | Replace the whole assistant message with a fixed notice, and drop its citations. An answer can quote or closely paraphrase its source, so stripping excerpts alone wouldn't remove the copied text |
| 3 | Past answers citing a document the student can no longer read, without it being deleted: withdrawn, taken down, or they left a course | The same as deletion, decided when the message is read rather than stored, so it follows the one read policy from M3-D |
| 4 | The legacy `POST /ai/chat`, which returns raw context passages (G-18) | Remove `context` from it now. Retire the route once conversations ship, since the mobile app won't use it |
| 5 | A conversation's title | The first question, cut to 80 characters at a word boundary. Not generated by a model, which would cost a call and could invent things |
| 6 | Retention | Still the open product decision Decision A recorded. **Conversations aren't released to students until it's settled**, and until then `expires_at` is null |

## Ownership and storage

Decision A applies: the backend issues the IDs and owns persistence, authorization, retention and deletion.

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| `conversations` | Tenant, owner, title, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `expires_at`, status |
| `conversation_messages` | Conversation, a sequence number, role, content, `grounded`, `redacted`, `created_at` |
| `conversation_citations` | Message, number, asset and source IDs, title, pages, excerpt |
| `conversation_turn_keys` | Conversation, idempotency key, the question's SHA-256, state (`pending` or `stored`), a lease, and the stored turn |

- **Owned by one user in one tenant.** Anyone else, staff included, gets `RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. There's no staff route to read a student's conversations; that would need its own decision, as break-glass access to documents would.
- **Order:** conversations by `updated_at DESC, id DESC`, and messages by their sequence number, so a question and its answer never swap even when their timestamps are equal.
- **Never logged or sent to analytics** (PRD §231). A test captures the logs during a turn and finds no message text in them.
- **The migration** takes the next Alembic number after M3-D's 0030 and L4's outbox, whichever order those merge in.

## Sending a message

`POST /ai/conversations/{id}/messages` with an `Idempotency-Key`:

1. **Reserve**, in a short transaction: lock the conversation, then insert the turn key as `pending` with a lease, or find it.
   - Already `stored` with the same question: return the stored turn, with no AI call.
   - The same key with a different question: `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`.
   - `pending` within its lease: 409 `TURN_IN_PROGRESS` with `Retry-After` (decision 1).
   - `pending` past its lease: take it over, since the earlier request died.
2. **Ask the AI service**, outside any transaction, over the existing signed call.
3. **Check every citation** (see [Citations](#citations)). If any fails, the answer becomes the fixed "not covered" reply with `grounded: false` and no citations, and the event is logged without content.
4. **Store**, in one transaction: lock the conversation. If it's been deleted, store nothing and return 404. Otherwise insert the question, the answer and its citations with the next two sequence numbers, mark the key `stored`, and update `updated_at` and the title if this is the first question.
5. **On failure** at step 2, `AI_UPSTREAM_ERROR` or `AI_UNAVAILABLE`, the key is deleted and nothing is stored. A retry asks again.

- **An uncertain timeout**, where the AI service may have answered but the backend didn't hear back, stores nothing. The retry asks again, which costs a call but can't leave a half-stored turn.
- **Two different keys at once** in one conversation are both answered. The conversation lock only orders their storage.
- **Each turn is answered on its own**, as the contract says. Passing history to the AI service is a separate AI contract change.
- **Rate limits** use the existing `ai_request` throttle, with the message limited to 4,000 characters.

## Citations

The boundary design requires that the backend never forwards model output blindly. For each citation, the backend checks, after generation:

- **The document exists in this tenant and the caller may read it now**, by M3-D's one read policy.
- **It isn't deleted, and its latest job is `ready`.** This catches a document that stopped being eligible while the answer was being generated.
- **The chunk belongs to that document's current run and attempt.** The AI service doesn't return the run and attempt today, so this needs the AI change below. Until then, the check is on the document only, and the gap is stated rather than assumed closed.

`source_id` and `title` come from the backend's own records, and pages from the AI service, nullable, never guessed.

**The excerpt needs an AI contract change.** The contract says the excerpt is "from the cited passage. Never generated". The AI service's citations carry no text, and its context items carry no chunk ID, so the backend has no reliable way to tie a passage to a citation, and guessing would break that rule. **The AI service adds the cited chunk's text, run and attempt to each citation**, in a PR M3-F owns. The backend cuts the text to 500 characters at a word boundary. Until that PR merges, no conversation route ships.

## Deletion and lost access

- **Deleting a conversation** removes its messages, citations and keys in one transaction. A turn finishing afterwards stores nothing. A repeat answers `RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, as the contract says.
- **A deleted document (ruling 9 on M3-D):** M3-D emits `content.document.deleted`, with IDs and no content. M3-F consumes it from the outbox and, in one transaction per message:
  - Replaces every assistant message citing it with a fixed notice: "This answer cited a document that's no longer available."
  - Sets `redacted: true` and deletes its citations, excerpts included.
  - Keeps the student's own question, which is their text, not the source's.
- **Lost access without deletion** (decision 3) is applied when messages are read: a message citing anything the caller can no longer read is returned redacted the same way. So it follows the read policy immediately, without needing an event for every way access can end.
- **Audit records** keep message and asset IDs and the time of redaction, never the removed text.
- **The AI service holds nothing** of a conversation: it's stateless per turn, and memory is gated off.

**Contract changes**, in M3-F's contract PR: `ConversationMessage` gains `redacted`, and `sendMessage` gains 409 `TURN_IN_PROGRESS`.

## Before and after M3-D and M4

| Stage | What answers may use | Citations checked against |
|---|---|---|
| Today | Every chunk in the tenant | Nothing: they're dropped |
| After M3-D's first PR | `institutional` chunks only | The backend's read policy, and `ready` |
| After M4 | The allow-list: the caller's own `ready` documents and the institutional ones they're eligible for | The same, plus run and attempt |

**Conversations can ship after M3-D's first PR**, answering from institutional material only. Students' own documents become usable in their answers with M4. M3-F's citation check is a second line behind M4's filter, not a replacement for it.

## Acceptance tests

On migrated PostgreSQL, with the AI service's test providers in the boundary gate for the AI paths.

| Requirement | Test must show |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Another student, staff, another tenant: `RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` on every route |
| Idempotency | The same key and question: one AI call, the same turn. A different question: `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`. Concurrent repeats: one call, the rest `TURN_IN_PROGRESS` |
| Abandoned turn | A request killed during the AI call: its lease expires, and a retry with the same key asks again and stores once |
| Failure | AI down or erroring: nothing stored, the key freed |
| Ordering | Questions and answers in sequence, even with equal timestamps and concurrent turns |
| Deleted mid-turn | A conversation deleted during the AI call stores nothing |
| Citations | Wrong tenant, unreadable, deleted, not `ready`, or made unavailable during generation: the whole answer becomes "not covered" (boundary) |
| Excerpt | Matches the cited chunk's text, at most 500 characters, never model output |
| No internals | No chunk IDs, storage paths, tenant IDs, `memory_used` or uncited context in any response |
| Redaction | After a document's deletion, every message citing it is redacted, with no excerpt left in the database. After lost access, it's redacted when read |
| Logs | No message text in the logs during a turn |
| Bad input | M3-C's PostgreSQL bounds: NUL, oversized offsets, a message past 4,000 characters |

## What this design doesn't settle

- **Retention**, which blocks release to students.
- **Follow-up questions** that understand earlier turns. That's an AI contract change, not in this contract version.
- **Staff access** to a student's conversations, which is not provided.
